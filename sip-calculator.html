The Mathematics of Mutual Fund SIP Compounding

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) is an investment vehicle offered by mutual funds to invest a fixed sum regularly into a scheme. SIP operates on two fundamental financial principles: Rupee Cost Averaging and Compound Interest.

FV = P × [((1 + i)^n - 1) / i] × (1 + i)

Where:

  • FV: Future Accumulated Value.
  • P: Monthly Contribution Amount.
  • i: Periodic Monthly Return Rate = (Annual Rate ÷ 12) ÷ 100.
  • n: Total Number of Months.

Frequently Asked Questions About SIPs

Rupee Cost Averaging is an automatic risk-mitigation feature of systematic investing. When financial markets are down, your fixed monthly allocation buys more mutual fund units at lower Net Asset Values (NAV). When markets rise, you purchase fewer units. Over time, this smooths out market volatility and lowers your average purchase cost per unit without requiring you to time the market.

Under revised tax rules for Equity Mutual Funds: Long-Term Capital Gains (LTCG on units held for > 12 months) are taxed at 12.5% on gains exceeding ₹1.25 Lakh per financial year. Short-Term Capital Gains (STCG on units held ≤ 12 months) are taxed at 20%. For Debt Mutual Funds, gains are taxed at your applicable income tax slab rate.
