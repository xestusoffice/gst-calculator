Mathematical Mechanics of Equated Monthly Installment (EMI)

An Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) is a fixed payment amount made by a borrower to a lender on a specified date of each calendar month. EMIs are applied to both interest and principal each month so that over a specified number of years, the loan is fully paid off.

EMI = [P × r × (1 + r)^n] / [(1 + r)^n - 1]

Where:

  • P: Principal Loan Amount.
  • r: Monthly Interest Rate = (Annual Interest Rate ÷ 12) ÷ 100.
  • n: Loan Tenure in Months = Number of Years × 12.

Frequently Asked Questions About Loan Prepayments

Choosing to reduce tenure saves significantly more interest over the long term. Because the compounding period of the remaining principal is shortened, interest accumulates for fewer years. However, if you are experiencing temporary cash-flow stress, opting to reduce monthly EMI can provide immediate monthly liquidity relief.

Paying just one extra EMI per year (13 payments instead of 12) on a 20-year home loan at 8.5% interest can shorten your entire loan tenure by approximately 4.5 to 5 years and save upwards of 20% to 25% of your total interest liability!
